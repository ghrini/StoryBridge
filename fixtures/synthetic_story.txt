Well, um, I grew up on a small farm outside of town. We had two cows, about thirty chickens, and a dog named Biscuit. Every morning at six o'clock I walked down the dirt road to wait for the school bus. It was a yellow bus, and it was always late. You know, I never minded, because my best friend Tommy waited with me, and we would count the crows on the fence. In the winter of 1958 the snow was so deep that the bus did not come for three days. We were not upset about that at all.

My first job was at the corner bakery. I was fourteen. Mr. Alvarez paid me one dollar a week, plus all the day-old bread I could carry home. Um, I was not a very good baker. I burned the first batch of rolls, and he did not yell at me. He just laughed and said, everybody burns the first batch. I worked there for two summers. I still remember the smell of cinnamon when I opened the door at five in the morning.

Every Sunday, my whole family came to my grandmother's house for dinner. There were eleven of us around one table, and the table was only built for eight. My uncle Joe always told the same joke, and we always laughed anyway. Well, my grandmother never let anyone leave without a plate of food to take home. I did not know it then, but those Sunday dinners were the best part of my childhood. If I could go back to one day, it would be one of those Sundays.
