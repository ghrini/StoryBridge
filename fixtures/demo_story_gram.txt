Well, um, when I was in high school, every morning I rode my bike to school. It was a blue bike with a basket on the front, and my brother Eddie fixed the chain more times than I can count. The school was two miles from our house, down Maple Hill. Going down that hill was the best part of the whole day. Coming home, you know, I had to push it all the way back up.

My first class was typing. Mrs. Kowalski was the teacher, and she was very strict. We had to type with a little cloth over our hands, so we couldn't look at the keys. I was not very fast at first, but by spring I could type forty words a minute. My best friend Dottie sat right next to me, and we passed notes whenever Mrs. Kowalski wasn't looking.

At lunch we sat on the front steps, if it wasn't raining. My mother packed me a sandwich, usually peanut butter, and an apple. Dottie always traded me her cookies for my apple. I never told my mother about that, not once.

After school I worked at the drugstore on Main Street, behind the soda counter. I made milkshakes for twenty five cents. Um, my favorite was the chocolate malt. I worked until six o'clock, and then I rode home in the dark with a little light on my handlebars. That was 1961. I didn't know it then, but those were some of the best days of my life.
