How it works

A recording is all it takesto keep a memory.

01 — Listen

Record a memory

With their permission, a volunteer records a grandparent telling a story. The recording stays on the volunteer's computer and is turned into text right there.

02 — Write

A picture book, not a transcript

AI helps turn what was said into short picture-book pages, and is told never to add anything they didn't say. Every sentence links back to the moment in the recording it came from.

03 — Read together

Ask for changes in your own words

The family reads every page. If something isn't right, they just say so, like "her friend's name is Dotty", and the story is rewritten. Nothing is shared until they confirm it.

04 — Share

Pictures, print, and share

Add photos or drawings to any page, flip through the book, then print it or send it to family.

For families

Their story stays theirs.

  • Nothing is recorded without the storyteller's permission, and they can stop or change their mind at any time.
  • The voice recording never leaves the volunteer's computer. It is turned into text on that computer.
  • Only if the storyteller agrees is the written text, never the voice, sent to an AI service to help draft the story.
  • Health details, addresses, money details, and other people's private information are kept out of the book.
  • Withdrawing deletes the recording, the text, and the book.
What's in a book

A keepsake made to be held.

  • Square 8 x 8 inch pages with a fun title, a picture box on every page, and big, easy-to-read words.
  • A print edition with small QR codes that play the storyteller's own voice (on the same Wi-Fi as the StoryBridge computer).
  • A family edition to send, and page pictures for photo-book websites.
